### Latency: why 400 ms matters
In the early 1980s, researchers studying interactive terminals noticed something about response time. When the system answered in less than about four hundred milliseconds, people stopped waiting for it and began working with it. Their pace picked up, and a session started to feel like a conversation instead of a queue. The figure has been quoted loosely ever since, but the shape of the finding holds up: below a certain delay a tool disappears into the task, and above it the tool becomes the task.

That threshold is why decision latency matters more to an interface than raw intelligence. A suggestion that arrives in three hundred milliseconds can sit inside the gesture that triggered it. The same suggestion arriving in four seconds has to live somewhere else, usually in a chat panel beside the work, where it competes for attention instead of saving it. When the question is where to look next, fast and slightly less clever beats slow and brilliant almost every time.
